## Non-negotiable growth laws
1. Understand the product before writing marketing claims.
2. Market only features that actually exist and work.
3. Define the audience and problem before selecting keywords or channels.
4. Optimize conversion and retention, not downloads alone.
5. Do not fake reviews, ratings, testimonials, scarcity, rankings, awards, or user counts.
6. Do not copy competitor names, screenshots, iconography, wording, or trade dress.
7. Respect store metadata, advertising, privacy, tracking, and consent rules.
8. Do not send spam or excessive notifications.
9. Every experiment needs a hypothesis, primary metric, guardrails, and decision rule.
10. Do not use vanity metrics as proof of product success.
11. Distinguish correlation from causation.
12. Consider low-volume statistical limitations.
13. Protect user trust and make cancellation, preferences, and opt-out clear.
14. Localize meaning, culture, screenshots, pricing context, and search behavior.
15. Report missing data and uncertainty honestly.

# Growth workflow

## Phase 0 — Product truth audit
Establish:
- what the product actually does today
- primary and secondary user segments
- the core job to be done
- first value moment
- strongest proof of value
- pricing, subscription, ads, and monetization
- current onboarding and retention mechanics
- platform, regions, languages, and age audience
- current analytics quality
- reviews, support issues, and known friction
- store listing and campaign assets

Do not build a strategy around planned features as if they already exist.

## Phase 1 — Market and competitor research
Analyze current competitors and substitutes across:
- positioning
- audience
- feature promise
- pricing and monetization
- category and keywords
- icons and screenshot narratives
- reviews: praised outcomes and repeated complaints
- onboarding and paywall timing
- content or community strategy
- credibility signals
- underserved needs

Look for white space, not merely common features.
Do not assume high ranking means high quality or profitability.

## Phase 2 — Positioning
Define:
- target audience
- high-value problem
- category frame
- unique mechanism or point of view
- primary benefit
- emotional payoff
- reasons to believe
- competitor alternative
- concise positioning statement
- message hierarchy

Create one clear promise rather than a list of every feature.

## Phase 3 — Growth model
Map the funnel:
- awareness
- store/product-page visit
- install
- first launch
- onboarding completion
- permission or account steps
- activation
- repeated core action
- retention
- referral
- monetization
- renewal or reactivation

Define a small set of metrics:
- north-star outcome
- acquisition efficiency
- listing conversion
- activation rate
- time to value
- cohort retention
- engagement quality
- conversion and renewal
- revenue and margin
- guardrails such as complaints, refunds, crashes, notification opt-outs, and deletion

# ASO strategy

## Keyword research
Build a region-specific keyword universe from:
- core category terms
- problem and intent terms
- outcome terms
- audience terms
- feature/mechanism terms
- competitor alternatives only where store rules permit and without misuse
- natural language from reviews, support, communities, and search suggestions

Evaluate:
- relevance
- search intent
- estimated demand
- competition/difficulty
- current ranking
- conversion potential
- localization meaning

Prioritize relevance and conversion over raw volume.
Avoid keyword stuffing and unnatural copy.

## Metadata architecture
For each platform and locale, prepare fields supported by current rules:
- name/title
- subtitle or short description
- keyword field where supported
- promotional text where supported
- full description
- release notes
- category choices
- tags/custom product page or listing variants where supported

Do not hardcode field limits without current verification.
Use benefits and natural language while placing priority terms strategically.

## Store visual narrative
The icon should:
- remain recognizable at small size
- use a distinct silhouette and focal point
- avoid tiny text
- express category and personality without looking derivative

The first screenshots should communicate:
1. what the app is
2. the main benefit
3. the unique interaction or proof

The full sequence should tell a journey, not show random screens.
Use accurate UI and truthful outcomes.
Design for region-specific language expansion and platform safe zones.

Preview video should show real product behavior, a clear hook, fast value demonstration, readable captions, and sound-off comprehension.

# Creative testing system
Create meaningful hypotheses around:
- audience angle
- problem angle
- emotional payoff
- product mechanism
- social proof
- visual world
- hook format
- demonstration versus lifestyle
- icon concept
- screenshot order and headline
- paywall framing

Change one strategic variable per test when possible.
Do not call minor color swaps a complete creative strategy.
Maintain a testing log with hypothesis, variant, audience, date, spend/exposure, result, confidence, and decision.

# Acquisition strategy
Choose channels based on audience and economics, such as:
- organic search and store discovery
- creator or influencer partnerships
- short-form social content
- community and referral
- content/SEO
- Apple Search Ads or other paid search
- social advertising
- partnerships and cross-promotion
- press and launch platforms
- email or owned audiences

For paid acquisition define:
- objective
- audience
- creative hypothesis
- landing/store destination
- budget guardrail
- attribution limitations
- target cost relative to realistic lifetime value
- stop/scale rule

Do not recommend spending before tracking, conversion, retention, and unit economics are minimally understood.

# Activation and onboarding
Improve activation by:
- demonstrating value before requesting commitment
- supporting guest mode where appropriate
- asking only essential questions
- using progressive permissions
- reducing time to first useful result
- personalizing only when it changes value
- explaining errors and recovery
- postponing paywall pressure until the user understands the benefit, unless the product is explicitly paid upfront

Measure activation as a meaningful user outcome, not “opened the app.”

# Retention and lifecycle
Build retention around recurring value, not notification pressure.
Define:
- natural use cadence
- habit or workflow trigger
- saved progress or personalization
- fresh content or changing utility
- meaningful goals, streaks, collections, or history where appropriate
- re-engagement based on user intent

For push/email/in-app messages specify:
- audience and eligibility
- event trigger
- timing and quiet hours
- frequency cap
- copy
- deep-link destination
- opt-out behavior
- success and guardrail metrics

Avoid guilt, fear, or manipulative streak-loss messaging.

# Monetization strategy
Evaluate:
- one-time purchase
- subscription
- freemium
- ads
- in-app purchases
- marketplace or transaction fee
- sponsorship
- enterprise or B2B

Align monetization with recurring value and user trust.
Define free value, premium value, trial, pricing logic, entitlement, upgrade, downgrade, cancellation, restore, refund, and win-back behavior.
Do not hide renewal or make cancellation harder than purchase.

# Ratings, reviews, and reputation
- Ask for ratings after a genuine positive outcome, not on first launch.
- Do not block features behind a positive review.
- Respond to reviews respectfully without exposing private data.
- Tag review themes and feed them into product priorities.
- Separate support resolution from review manipulation.
- Track rating distribution, volume, version, region, and recurring issues.

# Localization strategy
For each target market:
- research local search terminology
- adapt value proposition and cultural references
- localize screenshots and examples
- verify date, currency, units, names, and legal text
- consider local competitors and price expectations
- use native-quality review, not raw machine translation for high-value markets

# Analytics and experimentation
Define an event dictionary with:
- event name
- user meaning
- trigger
- properties
- privacy classification
- owner
- dashboard or decision use

Avoid collecting data without a decision purpose.
For experiments define:
- hypothesis
- population
- assignment unit
- primary metric
- guardrails
- duration or sample logic
- novelty and seasonality risks
- stopping and rollout rule

Do not repeatedly peek and stop at the first favorable fluctuation.

# Launch plan
Prepare phases:
1. Instrumentation and product readiness.
2. Small internal or trusted-user validation.
3. Beta/soft launch in controlled markets or audiences.
4. Listing and creative iteration.
5. Broader launch with support and monitoring.
6. Post-launch review and prioritized corrections.

Coordinate store release timing, content calendar, creator outreach, community, customer support, paid tests, and incident response.

# Definition of done
Growth work is complete only when:
- product truth and target audience are explicit
- positioning is differentiated and supportable
- keyword and metadata strategy is region-specific and current
- visual listing tells a clear, truthful story
- funnel metrics and event definitions support decisions
- experiments have hypotheses and guardrails
- acquisition recommendations fit retention and economics
- lifecycle messaging respects consent and frequency
- claims, assets, reviews, and attribution are ethical
- uncertainty and missing data are disclosed
